# Requirements: retvana engine baseline

## Introduction

This spec records how the retvana retirement model behaves, so the current behaviour is the contract
that future changes are measured against. It covers the projection engine (`src/retvana/engine.py`,
`src/retvana/vector.py`), the analyses built on it (`src/retvana/analysis.py`,
`src/retvana/property_model.py`), the app (`app.py`) and the Excel export (`src/retvana/export.py`).

It describes behaviour, not new features. Where the behaviour is a known simplification or a rule
inherited from the original spreadsheet the model was ported from, it is stated as-is and marked
**(inherited)** or **(simplification)**, so a later spec can change it on purpose. Known gaps are marked
as limitations.

Worked figures below are stated against the Example_Plan (`example_plan()` in `src/retvana/config.py`),
a synthetic household, or generically. Rates are 2026 Ontario values unless stated.

Conventions:
- **Person 1 / P1** and **Person 2 / P2** are the plan's two people, keys `'P1'` and `'P2'`. Person 1 is
  the first per-person block of the original spreadsheet's columns.
- **Projection year** = a calendar year from the retirement year to the end year. Balances in a year's row
  are opening balances.
- **Today's dollars** = deflated to the current year (2026) at the plan's inflation rate.
- **The spreadsheet settings** = a plan with the property mode, TFSA top-up, marginal-rate extra tax and
  life expectancy all off (the dataclass defaults, as in `example_plan()`). With these settings the
  engine reproduces the original spreadsheet; this was proved once during the open-source Migration by a
  parity check that is not part of this repository.
- **The app defaults** = `fresh_plan()` in `app.py`: the recovery property model, TFSA top-up,
  marginal-rate extra tax and life expectancy all on.

## Glossary

- **Engine:** `engine.project()`, the reference year-by-year projection for one plan.
- **Vector engine:** `vector.project_many()`, the same projection for many market and property paths at
  once, used by the Monte Carlo analyses.
- **Regression golden:** `scripts/regression_golden.json`, projection results of the Example_Plan for a
  set of cases, generated by `scripts/make_regression_golden.py` and compared by
  `scripts/regression_check.py`.
- **Estate after tax:** the value at the end of the plan, with registered balances taxed at the top rate
  and the corporation wound up (Requirement 17).
- **Money lasts:** total capital stays at or above one year of spending in every projection year.
- **Data_Dir:** the directory holding the user's `plan.json`: `RETVANA_DATA_DIR` if set, otherwise the
  platformdirs user data directory for `retvana`.

## Requirement 1: Plan configuration and persistence

**User story:** As a planner, I want every input in one plan object that I can save, load and share, so a
scenario is reproducible.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE system SHALL hold all inputs in a single `Plan` object with nested people, accounts, expenses,
   option grants, consulting contracts, properties, mortgages, property segments and legacy scenarios.
2. THE system SHALL serialize a plan to JSON and load it back to an equal plan (`Plan.to_json` /
   `Plan.from_json`). Unknown top-level keys SHALL be ignored, and missing keys SHALL take their
   defaults. **(limitation)** An unknown key inside a nested object (a person, account, etc.) raises an
   error.

   **Amended by nonreg-accounts:** one top-level key is not ignored. A non-zero `base_nonreg_p2`, written
   by plans saved before the nonreg-accounts spec, SHALL be converted on load into one Person 2
   non-registered account under nonreg-accounts Requirement 5.
3. WHEN the app starts AND `plan.json` exists in the Data_Dir, THE app SHALL load it. Otherwise it SHALL
   start from the app defaults. **(limitation)** In the baseline, a saved plan that could not be read
   stopped the app from starting, with no fallback. The open-source-release spec replaced this: the app
   now warns, starts from the app defaults and leaves the file untouched (`src/retvana/plan_io.py`).
4. WHEN the user clicks "Save as default", THE app SHALL write the current plan to `plan.json` in the
   Data_Dir. WHEN the user clicks "Reset", THE app SHALL restore the app defaults in the session only.
5. THE plan's end year SHALL be derived: the second death year when life expectancy is on, otherwise
   `fixed_end_year` (default 2080).

## Requirement 2: Starting position at retirement

**User story:** As a planner, I want the balances on the first day of retirement worked out from today's
balances, so the projection starts from a realistic position.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. FOR each account, of kind `registered`, `tfsa` or `nonreg`, THE engine SHALL grow today's balance
   to the retirement year as Excel `FV(rate, years, -contribution, -balance)`: contributions at year
   end. Accounts marked "TFSA limit" contribute the plan's TFSA limit ($7,000). Non-registered growth
   before retirement is not taxed. **(simplification)** *Amended by nonreg-accounts* (was: registered
   and TFSA accounts only).
2. THE engine SHALL compute the employee stock option net proceeds (`engine.options_net_proceeds()`) as
   Σ max(available × (`options_share_price` − grant price), 0) × (1 − 0.5 × `options_marginal_rate`), for
   all `option_grants`. The options belong to Person 1; this is a rule carried over from the original
   spreadsheet, not a plan setting.
3. THE engine SHALL compute the property surplus (Requirement 3). With N1 and N2 the sums of Person 1's
   and Person 2's non-registered accounts at retirement (criterion 1), it SHALL set:
   - Person 2's non-registered (`nonreg_p2`) = max(0, N2 + surplus);
   - Person 1's non-registered (`nonreg_p1`) = option proceeds + N1 + min(0, N2 + surplus): a shortfall
     beyond Person 2's non-registered money is funded from Person 1;
   - `shortfall_from_p1` = −min(0, N2 + surplus).

   *Amended by nonreg-accounts* (was: `base_nonreg_p2` in place of N2, and no N1).
4. THE engine SHALL inflate the yearly expenses from today's dollars to the retirement year at the expense
   CPI (`expense_cpi`, default 2.8%), separately for the go-go and later phases.
5. WITH the spreadsheet settings, THE starting values SHALL match the original spreadsheet's Inputs B8,
   B9, B11, B12, B16, B18, B19 and B23 to the cent. *(Verified once during the Migration by the
   spreadsheet parity check, which is not part of this repository.)*

## Requirement 3: Property sale and townhouse purchase

**User story:** As a planner, I want the sale of the three properties and the townhouse purchase modelled
with realistic costs, so I know what is left to invest.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE system SHALL value the properties and the townhouse on January 1 of the retirement year using the
   plan's property mode (Requirements 4 and 5).
2. THE system SHALL compute mortgage balances at sale with monthly compounding, one current term and one
   renewal rate for all later terms, matching a lender's amortization schedule to the dollar.
3. THE system SHALL deduct a real estate commission of 5% + 13% HST on the total sale value.
4. FOR each property that is not a principal residence (in the Example_Plan, Property 2 and Property 3),
   THE system SHALL compute capital gains tax as max(sale − ACB − selling costs, 0) × 50% inclusion ×
   the top rate (53.53%). The ACB is the purchase price unless set. *(simplification: top rate, single
   year)*
5. THE system SHALL compute the provincial and municipal land transfer tax on the townhouse price
   (`Waterfall.ltt_provincial`, `Waterfall.ltt_municipal`) from `ltt_brackets`, whose default provincial
   and municipal brackets are identical up to $3M. *(simplification: municipal luxury tiers above $3M not
   modelled)*
6. THE surplus SHALL be total sale − mortgages − commission − capital gains tax − land transfer tax −
   townhouse price, and MAY be negative.

## Requirement 4: Property values: simple scenarios (legacy)

#### Acceptance criteria
1. IN legacy mode, THE system SHALL grow every property and the townhouse by one factor
   (1 + recovery rate)^min(n, R) × (1 + long-run rate)^max(n − R, 0), where n = years from today.
2. THE scenarios SHALL be Bear (3 yrs at 0%, then 2.5%), Base (6%, 3.5%), Bull (10%, 4.5%) and Boom
   (11.5% throughout).
3. IF "townhouse follows the market" is off, THEN the townhouse SHALL stay at today's price.

## Requirement 5: Property values: recovery model and property Monte Carlo

**User story:** As a planner, I want each property type to recover toward its own fair value, with a range
of outcomes, so the plan reflects how different property types behave.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. FOR each asset, THE model SHALL evolve log value as
   log V[t+1] = log V[t] + ln(1 + g) + k·(log F[t] − log V[t]) + β·m[t] + σ·e[t], with fair value
   F[t] = target × 2022 peak × (1 + g)^t. The drift ln(1 + g) makes the value grow at the same
   (1 + g) rate as fair value.
2. THE shared market shock SHALL be autocorrelated:
   m[0] = σm·z[0], m[t] = φ·m[t−1] + σm·√(1−φ²)·z[t] (defaults σm = 7%, φ = 0.5).
3. WITH no shocks (n_paths = 0), THE model SHALL return the single median path used by the deterministic
   projection.
4. THE sale value SHALL apply the segment's sale discount (studio: 3%).
5. THE default segments SHALL be:
   - condo townhouse: target 1.00, speed 0.30, growth 3.5%, β 0.9, σ 5%;
   - condo apartment: 0.90, 0.20, 3.0%, β 1.1, σ 7%;
   - studio: 0.85, 0.15, 2.5%, β 1.2, σ 9%, discount 3%;
   - freehold townhouse: 1.00, 0.30, 4.0%, β 1.0, σ 5%.
6. THE property Monte Carlo SHALL use common random numbers across sale years: a fixed 40-year draw window
   and seed 2026.
7. THE overview projection in recovery mode SHALL use the actual simulated path whose surplus is the
   median, and the P10 and P90 paths for the capital range.

## Requirement 6: Yearly projection: spending and returns

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE engine SHALL project each year from the retirement year to the end year.
2. THE year's spending SHALL be the go-go amount while years since retirement < go-go years (15), otherwise
   the later amount, inflated at the plan inflation from the retirement year. After the first death it is
   multiplied by the survivor share (Requirement 10).
3. THE engine SHALL apply one return rate to all accounts in a year: the plan's return (5.5%), or the
   year's return from a supplied sequence (market Monte Carlo).
4. **(inherited)** Non-registered returns SHALL be treated as cash received in the year, with 75% of the
   return taxable every year.

## Requirement 7: Withdrawal order and surplus

**User story:** As a planner, I want the model to fund each year in a set order, so I can see where the
money comes from.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE shortfall SHALL be spending + income tax − (CPP + OAS + consulting + non-registered returns +
   planned registered draws), plus consulting CPP contributions and RRSP contributions, minus dividends
   and capital dividends from the corporation.
2. IF the shortfall is positive, THEN THE engine SHALL draw from, in order:
   1. non-registered, split by balance, **(inherited)** only if non-registered can cover the whole
      shortfall;
   2. TFSA, split by balance, if more than $0.01 remains, capped at the TFSA balance plus that year's
      return;
   3. extra registered withdrawals, split by registered balance.
3. **(inherited)** Extra registered withdrawals SHALL be taxed at the top rate (53.53%), or at the person's
   actual marginal rate when "accurate extra tax" is on.
4. IF the shortfall is negative (a surplus), THEN THE engine SHALL put it into each living person's TFSA
   room first when "TFSA top-up" is on. TFSA room = the TFSA limit indexed to inflation in $500 steps. The
   rest is reinvested in non-registered, using the original spreadsheet's cross-allocation formula while
   both are alive, or to the survivor.
5. **(simplification)** Capital cannot reach exactly $0: non-registered that cannot cover a whole year is
   never drawn. "Running out" is therefore tested as capital < one year of spending.

## Requirement 8: Income tax

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE engine SHALL tax each person separately on taxable income using 9 combined federal + Ontario
   brackets, with the cumulative tax at each threshold.
2. THE brackets and basic personal amount SHALL be indexed from 2026 to the retirement year when
   "index brackets" is on (default), and by inflation every later year.
3. THE tax SHALL be max(bracket tax − (BPA + additional personal amounts) × lowest rate − dividend tax
   credit, 0).
4. Taxable income SHALL be 75% of non-registered returns + other income (CPP, OAS, personal consulting) +
   the registered draws assigned to the person + 1.15 × non-eligible dividends − RRSP contributions − the
   CPP deduction (half of self-employed CPP). OAS enters net of the clawback. "Registered draws
   assigned to the person" means the planned draws after pension splitting. Extra registered draws
   are not part of this income; they are taxed separately (Requirement 7.3), as the person's tax with
   the draw minus the tax without it, or at the top rate.

## Requirement 9: RRIF minimums, pension splitting and withdrawal strategies

**User story:** As a planner, I want to compare ways of drawing down the RRSPs and RRIFs, so I can lower
lifetime tax and the OAS clawback.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE minimum withdrawal SHALL be the factor for the person's age (the year minus birth year) × the
   opening registered balance, from `RRIF_FACTORS`: 0 below 55, 1 / (90 − age) from 55 to 70, then a
   table from 7.38% at 71 to 20% from 94. **(inherited)** These are the factors from before 2015, as in
   the original spreadsheet. The current CRA factors are lower (for example 5.28% at 71 and 6.82% at 80),
   so the model forces larger RRIF withdrawals from 71 than the law requires.
2. Person 1's RRIF SHALL start at retirement. Person 2's SHALL start at the later of retirement and age 55
   (LIRA unlock, `rrif_min_age`). In the Example_Plan, Person 2 is 52 at retirement and the RRIF starts
   three years later.
3. WITH the "minimums" strategy, planned draws SHALL equal the minimums.
4. WITH the "defer" strategy, minimums SHALL start the year after the conversion age (71). Before that the
   planned draw is 0.
5. WITH the "bracket" strategy, through the fill-until age the planned draw SHALL be
   max(minimum, min(balance + growth, target × inflation index − other taxable income)), where the target
   is in today's dollars. After that age the draw is the minimum, and minimums start at 72, as under
   "defer". So with fill-until 64, nothing is planned at ages 65–71.
6. WHEN income splitting is on AND both are alive AND the person is 65+, THE engine SHALL split RRIF
   income using the original spreadsheet's formula.
7. THE strategy search SHALL try targets from $50K to $210K in $10K steps, with fill-until ages 64, 67 and
   71, and rank them by estate after tax.

## Requirement 10: Life expectancy and survivor rules

**User story:** As a planner, I want the plan to end at the second death and handle the first death
properly, so estate and lasting money are realistic.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. WHEN life expectancy is on, each person SHALL be alive through the end of birth year + death age
   (`Person.death_age`, dataclass default 95; the Example_Plan uses 92 for Person 1 and 96 for Person 2).
2. AFTER the first death:
   - the deceased's balances SHALL roll to the survivor in full (non-registered, registered, TFSA), tax-free;
   - the deceased's CPP, OAS, tax and consulting SHALL be zero;
   - income splitting SHALL stop;
   - spending SHALL be multiplied by the survivor share (70%).
3. THE survivor's CPP SHALL be max(own, min(own + rate × the deceased's pension at 65, the maximum CPP
   indexed from $1,507.65/month in 2026)), with a rate of 60% at 65+ or 37.5% under 65.
   *(simplification: under-65 flat-rate part and death benefit not modelled)*
4. IF Person 2 dies first, THEN Person 1 SHALL inherit the corporation's shares.
5. *(Verified by `survivor_check.py`.)*

## Requirement 11: CPP and OAS

#### Acceptance criteria
1. CPP SHALL be the estimate at 65 × 12, indexed at CPP inflation from the year the person turns 65 ×
   the early/late factor (−7.2%/yr before 65, +8.4%/yr after, ages 60–70) × the contributory factor.
2. THE contributory factor SHALL be min(1, (years from 18 to retirement + consulting years before 65 pro
   rata to the YMPE) / 39), when proportional CPP is on.
3. OAS SHALL be $751.97/month (2026) indexed at inflation, +10% from 75, × (1 + 7.2% × years deferred,
   65–70).
4. THE OAS clawback SHALL be min(15% × max(previous year's taxable income − threshold, 0), OAS), with the
   threshold $95,323 (2026) indexed to the income year (the previous year). Previous year's income
   includes extra registered withdrawals when "accurate extra tax" is on.

## Requirement 12: Consulting income (personal)

#### Acceptance criteria
1. EACH contract SHALL have an owner, an amount in today's dollars (optionally indexed), first and last
   years, and an RRSP option. It stops at the owner's death.
2. Self-employed CPP SHALL be 11.9% of earnings between $3,500 and the YMPE plus 8% between the YMPE and
   the YAMPE (2026: $74,600 / $85,000, indexed). It is paid while age < 70 and (age < 65 or age < CPP start
   age). Half is deducted from income and half is a credit at the lowest rate.
3. WITH the RRSP option, THE contribution SHALL be min(18% of last year's consulting, the RRSP limit
   indexed from $33,810), through age 71. It is deducted from income and added to registered next year.
4. *(Verified by `consulting_check.py`.)*

## Requirement 13: Consulting corporation

#### Acceptance criteria
1. Contracts marked "through my corporation" (`via_corp`) SHALL be corporate revenue: no CPP
   contributions, no CPP years, no RRSP room.
2. Active income SHALL be taxed at 11.2% up to the small-business limit ($500K, reduced by $5 per $1 of
   last year's taxable investment income over $50K), then at 26.5%.
3. Corporate investments SHALL earn the plan return. The taxable 75% is taxed at 50.17%, of which 30.67% is
   added to RDTOH. The untaxed 25% is paid out the same year as a tax-free capital dividend.
   *(simplification)*
4. Dividends SHALL be non-eligible: each dollar recovers 38.33¢ of RDTOH (capped at the balance), is
   grossed up 15% personally, and earns a credit of 11.0201% of the grossed-up amount (federal 9.0301% +
   Ontario 1.99%).
5. THE dividend policy SHALL be either "fill each eligible shareholder's taxable income to a target (today's
   $)" or "pay everything out", limited to what the corporation can pay.
6. Person 1 SHALL receive dividends only IF Person 1 holds shares (`p1_shareholder`) AND (Person 1 has a
   corporate contract that year OR Person 2 is 65+), OR Person 1 has inherited the shares (TOSI).
7. AT the end of the plan, THE corporation SHALL be valued as a final dividend taxed at the top
   non-eligible rate.
8. *(Verified by `corp_check.py`.)*

## Requirement 14: Market risk Monte Carlo

#### Acceptance criteria
1. EACH path SHALL have one standard-normal shock per calendar year (seed 1971), shared by every retirement
   year compared.
2. THE retirement return SHALL be exp(ln(1 + r) + σ·z) − 1, so the median equals the plan's return. Each
   account's pre-retirement return, non-registered accounts included, uses its own rate and
   volatility, driven by the same shock. *Amended by nonreg-accounts.*
3. IN recovery mode, path i SHALL pair market path i with property path i.
4. THE first-year stress test SHALL add the chosen move to every path's first retirement-year return.
5. Success SHALL mean the money lasts (glossary). THE app SHALL report the success rate, the capital
   bands, the chance of running out by year, and spending at 90% and 75% success with nothing left over.

## Requirement 15: Retirement-year comparison

#### Acceptance criteria
1. FOR each year in the chosen range, THE system SHALL report:
   - capital at retirement;
   - first-year spending and draw (the draw is the first-year expense / capital ratio);
   - capital in 2050, 2065 and at the end;
   - lifetime tax;
   - in recovery mode: property surplus P10/P50/P90, the chance of a shortfall, and end capital P10/P90;
   - with market risk on: the success rate and end capital P10/P50.

## Requirement 16: Sustainable spending solver

**User story:** As a planner, I want the most I can spend while the money lasts and I leave a chosen
estate, so I can set a spending level with confidence.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE solver SHALL scale all spending, or discretionary items only, by one factor in every year and
   phase.
2. WITH a fixed return, THE answer SHALL be the largest scale (bisection on 0–4×) where the money lasts
   AND the estate after tax in today's dollars ≥ the target.
3. WITH market risk, THE answer SHALL be the largest scale where the share of histories meeting both tests
   ≥ the confidence. THE app SHALL also show the typical (P50) and bad-case (P10) estate at that scale.
4. THE answer SHALL sit at the edge: spending 0.2% more fails the goal. *(Verified by `spending_check.py`.)*
5. "Apply to my plan" SHALL multiply the expense items by the scale.

## Requirement 17: Estate after tax

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE estate after tax SHALL be non-registered + TFSA + registered × (1 − top rate) + the corporation's
   wind-up value, using the final year's opening balances. *(simplification: no deemed disposition of
   non-registered gains, no probate)*
2. THE app SHALL show it in today's dollars, as well as in end-year dollars.

## Requirement 18: Excel export

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE export SHALL be a values-only workbook with these sheets: Summary (key inputs and results, with a
   chart of total capital and expenses from the Cash flow data), Cash flow, Compare years, Property,
   Inputs, and Market risk (with its own chart) and Withdrawals when they apply.
2. THE Summary SHALL include the life expectancy, withdrawal strategy, market risk and spending solver
   settings and results.

## Requirement 19: App behaviour and deployment

#### Acceptance criteria
1. THE app SHALL apply every input widget before the model runs, so results always match the inputs on
   screen.
2. THE app SHALL follow the OS light/dark appearance (or the Settings choice) for the theme, charts and
   cards.
3. THE app SHALL be reachable only on localhost: Streamlit `server.address` is localhost, and Docker
   publishes on `127.0.0.1:8501`. The container runs as non-root with a read-only filesystem, no
   capabilities, and the host Data_Dir mounted at `/data` for saved plans.
4. `make up | down | rebuild | logs | status` SHALL manage the container.

## Requirement 20: Verification

**User story:** As a maintainer, I want automatic checks that prove the model still behaves as specified,
so any change that breaks a rule is caught.

#### Acceptance criteria
1. WITH the spreadsheet settings, THE engine matched the original spreadsheet's golden master on every
   compared Projections value, within $0.01 (28,000+ values, 8 cases). This was a one-time Migration
   check and is not part of this repository. In this repository, THE engine SHALL match the regression
   golden on every column of every case within its stored tolerances. *(`regression_check.py`)*
2. THE vector engine SHALL match the engine within a relative difference of 1e-9 on every compared column,
   across all strategy, survivor, consulting and corporate cases, with fixed and random returns, including
   depletion. With zero volatility, the market Monte Carlo SHALL equal the deterministic plan.
   *(`vector_check.py`)*
3. THE survivor, spending, consulting and corporate rules SHALL each have a hand-calculation check
   (`survivor_check.py`, `spending_check.py`, `consulting_check.py`, `corp_check.py`).
4. THE app SHALL pass a headless smoke test that drives every tab's main controls and the Excel export.
   *(`smoke_test.py`)*
5. ALL of the above SHALL pass before a change is considered done.
